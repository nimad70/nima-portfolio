## The problem

Diabetic retinopathy is a leading cause of preventable blindness, and it is
diagnosed by looking at retinal photographs. Screening is a volume problem:
enough images, not enough specialists to grade them, and the earliest stages
are the subtlest to spot.

## Approach

Transfer learning from Inception V3, fine-tuned on the APTOS 2019 Blindness
Detection dataset to classify images into five severity grades.

Most of the work was not the model. Retinal photographs vary enormously in
illumination, contrast and framing between cameras and clinics, and that
variation swamps the signal. The preprocessing pipeline mattered more than
architecture choice:

- CLAHE for local contrast, so lesions survive uneven illumination
- Gaussian blur to suppress sensor noise before edge extraction
- Sobel and Canny edges to sharpen vessel and lesion boundaries

## Results

**79% accuracy** across five grades after fine-tuning and hyperparameter
search.

## The number in context

Five-class accuracy flattens something important: the classes are ordered, and
the errors are not equally costly. Confusing grade 0 with grade 1 is minor;
confusing grade 0 with grade 4 is a missed emergency. Accuracy treats those
identically.

Quadratic weighted kappa — the metric the APTOS competition actually used —
penalises distant confusions more heavily and is the right measure here. If I
revisited this, that is the first thing I would change, along with reporting
per-class recall so the behaviour on rare severe cases is visible rather than
averaged away.

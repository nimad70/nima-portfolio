## The problem

Text-only assistants read what you typed, not how you are. A user who is
frustrated and one who is curious can send the same words and get the same
reply, which is usually the wrong reply for at least one of them.

## Approach

Two systems joined at the interface:

- **Vision** — ResNet SSD for face detection, EfficientNetB0 for emotion
  classification, running per-frame on a webcam stream through OpenCV.
- **Conversation** — a Hugging Face model orchestrated with LangChain, taking
  the detected emotional signal as additional context alongside the message.

The Streamlit interface exposes the parts that usually stay hidden: live
bounding boxes, the classifier's confidence, and a threshold control. Being
able to watch the classifier disagree with you is most of what makes the
system debuggable.

For deployment, EfficientNetB0 is converted to TensorFlow Lite with float16
quantisation, cutting model size substantially at negligible accuracy cost.
The whole application is containerised so it runs the same way anywhere.

## Status

In progress. Vision and interface work; the emotion signal is wired into the
conversation but the prompting strategy around it is still being tuned.

## The honest difficulty

Facial expression is a weak proxy for emotional state. It varies by person,
culture and context, and datasets in this space carry well-documented bias.
Treating a classifier output as ground truth about someone's feelings would be
both wrong and a bit rude. The design question I am still working on is how to
use a low-confidence signal usefully — nudging tone rather than asserting that
it knows how you feel.

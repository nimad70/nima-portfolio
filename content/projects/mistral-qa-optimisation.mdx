## The problem

General-purpose models answer medical questions confidently and are wrong often
enough to matter. The gap is not reasoning so much as domain grounding: the
model has seen medical text, but not enough of it, and cannot tell which of its
recollections are reliable.

## Approach

Two interventions, measured separately so their contributions could be told
apart:

- **Fine-tuning** — Mistral 7B v0.2 adapted on curated medical QA datasets
  (MedQuAD, MedicalQA) using LoRA and QLoRA, keeping training tractable on
  limited hardware.
- **Retrieval** — a RAG pipeline supplying domain passages at inference, using
  RAFT so the model is trained to work with retrieved context rather than
  merely having it appended.

RAFT matters here. A model fine-tuned without retrieved context, then given
retrieved context at inference, tends to ignore it. Training with the retrieval
distribution it will actually see teaches it to use the documents.

## Results

Accuracy on held-out questions rose from **84% to over 95%**.

## Caveat

That number is accuracy on a benchmark, not clinical safety. Benchmark QA
rewards matching a reference answer; it says nothing about behaviour on
questions outside the distribution, or about whether the model declines when it
should. I would not put this in front of a patient, and the interesting
follow-up work is measuring calibration and refusal rather than pushing
accuracy higher.

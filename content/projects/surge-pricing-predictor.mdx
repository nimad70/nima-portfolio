## The problem

Predicting when hourly market prices will spike, from historical pricing
patterns and movement trends.

Precision matters more than recall for this. A missed surge costs an
opportunity; a false alarm costs a decision made on bad information. The models
were tuned accordingly.

## Approach

A comparison across model families rather than a commitment to one:
XGBoost, random forests, SVMs and small neural networks, all on the same
features and splits so the differences were attributable to the models.

Principal component analysis handled dimensionality reduction and feature
selection, which mattered because the raw feature set was wide relative to the
number of usable hourly observations.

## Results

**76% precision** from the best-performing configuration.

## Honest limitations

This is a time series, and time series make it very easy to fool yourself. The
things I would want to verify before trusting this number:

- That the splits are strictly chronological, with no future information
  reachable from the training window
- That features are computed from data available at prediction time, not
  aggregated across the whole series
- That the baseline is a realistic one — "predict no surge" is a strong
  baseline when surges are rare, and beating it is the only result worth
  reporting

Without those confirmed, 76% precision is a promising signal rather than a
finding. I include it because the modelling comparison was sound; I would not
put money behind the number.
